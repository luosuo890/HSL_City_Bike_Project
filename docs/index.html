Methodological Guidelines & Operational Boundaries for Commuter Analysis:
• Europe/Helsinki Local Time: All timestamps and hourly bins strictly reflect local Finnish time, correctly capturing morning (07:00–09:00) and evening (16:00–18:00) commuter schedules.
• Day-Type Definitions: "Weekday (Mon–Fri)" and "Weekend (Sat–Sun)" are categorized by calendar day-of-week. Public holidays falling on weekdays are preserved in the Monday–Friday pool without arbitrary exclusion.
• Normalized Daily Averages: All diurnal volume figures are divided by the exact number of operating days for that day-type within the target timeframe, preventing misinterpretation from unequal weekday vs. weekend calendar days.
• Descriptive Patterns vs. Dispatch Instructions: Intraday net flows reflect passenger choice and directional drift across completed journeys. They identify candidate priority corridors for pre-staging, but do NOT prove causal decision drivers or constitute automated truck dispatch orders without live 15-minute dock sensors.

Methodological Guidelines & Operational Boundaries for Weather Analysis:
• Retrospective Ground Observations: Weather metrics represent historical measurements from FMI official stations (Kaisaniemi and Tapiola). They are strictly NOT weather forecasts and cannot be used for prospective day-ahead dispatch planning.
• Descriptive Correlation vs. Causality: Observed positive temperature correlation (+0.74 to +0.78 within cities) reflects seasonal co-movement heavily confounded by solar daylight duration (5.5 hrs in winter to 19 hrs in midsummer) and school/academic vacation schedules. These statistical associations must NOT be interpreted as causal ridership drivers.
• Strict Model Feature Exclusion: To maintain strict operational integrity, weather variables were intentionally excluded from our 2025 machine learning predictive models, preventing reliance on synthetic or forecasted meteorological data.
• Unmapped Docks Integrity: 23,981 journeys departing from 7 unmapped docks (e.g., temporary workshop stations) are fully retained in city-wide daily aggregates, ensuring zero trip loss while preserving map coordinate rigor.

Methodological Guidelines & Operational Boundaries for Demand Forecasting:
• Single Out-of-Time Backtest: All metrics reflect a single evaluation cycling season (2025). The lower volume error of the Same-Month Baseline and higher ranking overlap of Ridge regression serve as empirical planning observations, not claims of permanent or statistically significant superiority.
• Demand Forecast Errors: MAE measures trips, not bicycles. Error metrics (MAE and RMSE) are strictly denominated in completed passenger journeys (trips) per station-month, not physical bicycle inventory. Bicycles turn over multiple times per day across stations.
• Flow Terminology & Rebalancing Limits: Terms such as "Net Outflow" (returns less than departures) and "Net Inflow" (returns greater than departures) quantify directional journey imbalances over 30 days. They do not represent physical dock fullness or empty states, and must not be treated as automated rebalancing truck dispatch orders.
• No Weather Feature Leakage: Predictive models were trained strictly using historical lag volumes, geospatial coordinates, dock capacities, and month indicators. Actual 2025 weather was completely omitted from model feature matrices.
• Strict Cohort Differentiation: Full-Season Baseline (N = 3,170 station-months, April to October) includes April season opening data; Multi-Model Test (N = 2,718 station-months, May to October) omits April because 1-month lag models require previous-month operating history. These cohorts must never be merged.

Essential Methodological Boundaries for Transport Planners & Operators:
• Net Flow Definition: Net flow equals returns minus departures, reflecting 30-day cumulative directional passenger drift across stations. It does not represent instantaneous dock capacity, real-time bicycle inventory, or truck redistribution requirements.
• Historical Weather Observations: Sourced retrospectively from Finnish Meteorological Institute (FMI) ground stations (Kaisaniemi and Tapiola). Strictly historical observations, not weather forecasts; observed correlations reflect seasonal co-movement and are non-causal.
• Demand Forecast Errors: MAE measures trips, not bicycles. Error is quantified in completed passenger journeys per station-month, not physical missing bicycles, because bicycles circulate across multiple journeys daily.
• Out-of-Time Backtest: Evaluated across a single out-of-time test season (2025). Serves as an exploratory operational planning benchmark without claims of statistical significance.
